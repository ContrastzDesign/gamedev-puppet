Content Short Skill Flow

How the skill turns one script tab into a finished, captioned 24-fps Short. SKILL.md runs eleven steps in order and opens a reference file only at the step that needs it. The review and the z-fighting pass loop back until they pass, and nothing renders on your Mac before that.

What happens at each step

Prep steps 1–3

  1. 1

    Lock the script

    Read the tab list in the Scripts for Game Dev Puppet Google Doc. A tab whose name starts with ✅ is already posted, so it's skipped. Take the first unticked tab unless you name another, and lock the script exactly as written: no fact-checking and no rewriting.

    If it fails A ✅ tab is skipped; move on to the next tab.

  2. 2

    Voice-over

    Generate the paid voice-over with the approved credentials. Its transcript and word timings decide when every beat lands and how long the film runs. Timings are never estimated from a duration.

  3. 3

    Beat map

    Tag every sentence with its rung on the problem ladder (naive version, problem, fix, mechanism, name, title answer, callback, congratulations, kicker). Then look up its picture, signal colour, camera move, caption span and cast reaction. Something new appears every 24–48 frames. This map stays internal: there's no storyboard review.

    Reads
    • references/script-ladder.md
    • references/script-writing.md §4

Build steps 4–7

  1. 4

    World and props

    Pick one world kit: the workshop desk for most game-dev topics, the field for outdoor and physical ones. The hero object starts at its naive version and gains one part per fix. Props are built from primitives; Poly Pizza models are allowed only once they're converted to the toon look.

    Reads
    • references/style.md
  2. 5

    Cast

    Cast three household objects from one room that suits the topic, with at most one accessory for the whole cast, and build them with the googly-eye rig. Their pupils point at whatever the beat is about: the lime part, the problem, or the camera.

    Reads
    • references/characters.md
    • assets/googly-cast.html
  3. 6

    Camera

    Long, overlapping, eased moves of 72–192 frames: orbits, push-ins through x-ray walls and pull-backs, cycling through wide, medium and close. The camera only stops on the deliberate freeze, and the film ends on its opening composition.

    Reads
    • references/style.md
  4. 7

    Captions and sound

    Keyword captions come straight from the VO word timings, automatically. Sound effects are real recordings placed on meaningful actions, with the VO on top. Songs and music beds are downloaded with yt-dlp, and only when they're free to use or licensed.

    Reads
    • references/audio-captions.md

Check steps 8–9

  1. 8

    Review the editable film

    Render stills at every beat start, every lime, red and mint moment, every camera landing and caption change, and the first and last frames. Check each against the quality gate in SKILL.md and fix what's wrong in the editable scene. Short motion clips are allowed for pupils, pops and dust; a full draft movie isn't.

    Reads
    • assets/style-guide.html

    If it fails Fix it in the editable scene, then render those stills again.

  2. 9

    Final pass: z-fighting

    The last check before the render, because remodelling can break a pose or a contact. Run z-audit.js at each beat start until it reports nothing. Then look at close, wide and grazing-angle stills for flicker, stripes, dotted outlines and flipped faces, and render one short clip of the slowest camera move.

    Reads
    • assets/z-audit.js
    • references/characters.md

    If it fails Remodel the geometry, then audit again: sink solid parts, lift decals with polygonOffset, and stand outlined parts proud.

Ship steps 10–11

  1. 10

    Final render on the Mac

    Copy a self-contained render package to your MacBook Air and render the whole film as a lossless 1080×1920, 24 fps image sequence. Then encode it there with ffmpeg (H.264).

    Reads
    • references/mac-render.md

    If it fails If the Mac is offline, report it and wait. The render never moves to another machine.

  2. 11

    Verify and deliver

    Check the size, 24 fps, frame count and audio length with ffprobe, and look at decoded frames from the opening, a lime beat, a red beat, the freeze and the ending. Then deliver the file. The ✅ goes on the script tab only once the Short is actually posted.

Rules that hold at every step

  • One Short at a time. Reuse the current project and tools; no extra platforms or tooling.
  • Exactly 24 fps. Shots, captions, animation, render and ffmpeg all run on the integer frame number.
  • No storyboard review. The beat map and shot plan stay internal.
  • Captions, always. Automatic keyword captions, without asking.
  • Real sounds only. No generated or default SFX. Silence beats filler.
  • Fix before the render. Feedback is handled in the editable scene; the full render happens last, on your Mac.
  • Signal colours are reserved. Lime names a part, red is the problem, mint means solved.
  • Real objects, googly eyes. Built in code from primitives. The pupils do the acting.
  • The camera never parks. It only holds on the deliberate freeze, and the last frame matches the first.

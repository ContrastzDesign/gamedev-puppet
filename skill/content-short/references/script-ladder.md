# Scripts: the problem ladder

From the script teardown of all 35 shirusekaii Shorts (claude.ai artifact `CDMpmsEr9ha7twCCN6YMj2`).

**Ricardo's scripts are locked.** Use this file to read a script and stage it, never to rewrite it. Write or suggest a script only when Ricardo asks for one.

## The format

23 of the channel's 35 scripts use one structure, and it carries 78% of the views. Each rung has four parts, and almost every sentence either opens a problem or closes one.

1. **The naive version:** "If you [do the simplest thing], you've got [a THING]." It lands 2–4 s in, and it's technically true and a little funny.
2. **Break it,** as a concrete little scene: "But / Except / Until / Then [it fails in a specific, often funny way]."
3. **Fix it,** with the physics in one or two sentences: "So you [add a part]. [How it works.]"
4. **Name the part only after the viewer needed it:** "That's the [technical term]."
5. **Repeat 5–11 times.** Each fix should cause or reveal the next problem ("Better wind, bigger problem.").
6. **Close** with a real-world callback and the signature line: "So next time you [see it]... And congratulations, you just built a [THING]." Then a kicker.

**Pacing (the reference channel's):** a new problem every 15–25 s, 170–214 words a minute (median 201), and 100–170 s per film. The title's answer is held for 50–80% of the way through, as one rung. Ricardo's own scripts are much shorter (about 100 words, 30–45 s, three walls at most); see `script-writing.md`.

## Script beats to screen

This is the core of the beat map (step 3 in `SKILL.md`). Every beat produces one visible change. Tag each sentence and look up its treatment here; devices and timings are in `style.md`.

| Beat | Typical words | Picture | Cast | Caption span |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | first 7 frames | The finished hero explodes apart (about 17 frames), and then the naive version drops in with dust | The cast drops in and looks at the naive version | n/a |
| Naive version | "If you..., you've got a [THING]" | Only the simplest parts on screen. Medium shot, slow orbit. | Pupils on the object | "[THING]" |
| Problem | "But / Except / Until / Then..." | Act the failure as a tiny physical scene. Tint the faulty part red, or add a red arrow or red zone. | Tilt back, pupils snap to the problem | The problem words, **in red** |
| Self-inflicted problem | "Better wind, bigger problem." | The part that just went lime now goes red, so the cause is visible | A double-take: the pupils flick between the part and the camera | The problem words, in red |
| Fix | "So you [add PART]" | The new part pops in (7 frames, dust puff). Push in through an x-ray wall if it's inside. | Lean in | The part, if it's spoken |
| Mechanism | One or two sentences of physics | An inset bubble, force arrows (lime and blue), rods or cones, comparison arcs, or a counter | Watch it | One key phrase |
| Name | "That's the [TERM]." | Lime flash on exactly that part (up 4, hold 12–24, fade 7) | Every pupil on the part | **The term, verbatim** |
| Analogy | "like a hose that's been stepped on" | The analogy prop in the same world and render, never a photo or clip art | A cast member can play the analogy prop | The analogy noun |
| Number at human scale | "two and a half bathtubs" | A scale object plus a data-blue counter | Look at the scale object | The number with its unit |
| Time skip | "A few months go by..." | A light or weather change on the same set (like the storm variant), or a cut to a new map | A slow look around | The time phrase |
| Failed attempt | "Why not just...?" | The attempt fails, then the rewind badge plays it backwards | Pupils to the camera | The shortcut words |
| Title's answer | Lands at 50–80% | **Freeze:** all motion stops and the camera holds still. This is the only legal hold. Then lime on the answer part. | Frozen, eyes on the part | The answer phrase |
| "Next time you..." | The callback | Pull back to wide, framed like the real-world moment | Look at the camera | The callback noun |
| "Congratulations, you just built a [THING]" | The sign-off | The whole system flashes lime briefly, then the mint tick draws on with the green tint. This is the only mint in the film. | A group hop | "you just built a [thing]" |
| Kicker | A joke, warning or reframe | A signature character gag, then the sparkle dissolve back to the opening composition | The gag | The punch word |

**When a script doesn't follow the ladder** (a fact or study Short, a list), still give each sentence one visible change. Treat claims as fixes, objections as problems, and terms as names. Keep the colour meanings strict.

## Spin-off formats

- **Pet-care build:** "If you put a [pet] in a [tank], it has a home. Except..." Each rung is a health risk. It adds a time skip that reveals a slow problem, then a recap list before the kicker. Show the recap as the named parts flashing lime one after another.
- **"Why can't we just...?" teardown:** the viewer tries an obvious shortcut and hits a wall each time, with a bigger number at every wall. Each wall is a red beat with a counter; each failed attempt is a rewind badge. It ends on an aphorism. This is the format Ricardo's short scripts use; its beat-by-beat staging is in `script-writing.md`, section 4.
- **Scale ladder:** the viewer is the unit of measurement. Use scale objects and counters, with the twist in the last 30 seconds.
